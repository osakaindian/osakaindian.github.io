Getting a phone plan is one of the first things you'll need to sort out after arriving in Japan, and the options can feel overwhelming. The good news: once you understand how the market is layered, the choice gets much easier.

## How the market works

Japan has **four companies that own and run their own networks**: docomo, au (KDDI), SoftBank, and Rakuten Mobile. Everyone else either rents capacity from them or is a cheaper brand owned by one of them. That gives you four types of provider:

- **Main carriers** run their own networks and sell full-service plans, usually with shops, wide coverage, and extra services.
- **Online brands** (ahamo, LINEMO) are lower-cost plans from the big carriers. Signup and support happen online, with no shop visits.
- **Sub-brands** (UQ mobile, Y!mobile) are the big carriers' budget lines. They use the parent network and often keep physical shops.
- **MVNOs** rent network capacity and compete on price and flexibility. Some are aimed specifically at international residents.

## Which one should you choose?

- **Just arrived and need a phone fast?** A foreigner-oriented provider usually offers English signup and flexible paperwork, which helps before you've set up a Japanese bank account.
- **Tight budget?** Look at MVNOs like IIJmio or mineo, especially if you mostly use Wi-Fi at campus and home.
- **Heavy data user or commuter who needs a stable connection?** Consider ahamo, LINEMO, or a main carrier.
- **Want to walk into a shop and talk to someone?** Try UQ mobile, Y!mobile, or a main carrier store.

## Before you sign up

1. **Check your phone.** Make sure it's SIM-unlocked and supports Japanese network bands. eSIM support also varies by phone and provider.
2. **Know what you need to apply.** Usually a residence card and passport, and sometimes a Japanese address, bank account, or credit card, depending on the provider.
3. **Read the contract terms.** Look at minimum contract periods and early cancellation fees, since these differ widely.
4. **Compare total cost, not just the headline price.** Include calling fees, data limits, and any add-on charges.
5. **Test coverage where you live.** Network quality can differ between campus, your apartment, and your commute.

*Plans, prices, and English support change often, so confirm current details on the provider's website before applying.*
