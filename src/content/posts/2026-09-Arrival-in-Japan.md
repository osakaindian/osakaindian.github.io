Arriving in Japan comes with a surprising amount of paperwork, and most of it has to happen at a city office where the forms are in Japanese. The good news is that the steps follow a fixed order, and once the first one is done, the rest unlock one after another.

This checklist is written for Indian students and residents settling in Osaka and Nara. Keep all your documents in one folder and bring the whole set to every visit.

## The big picture

1. **At the airport:** collect your residence card.
2. **At city hall, within 14 days of moving in:** register your address, then enroll in health insurance.
3. **After that:** My Number, a Japanese SIM, a bank account, and your pension and tax status.
4. **Whenever you have a spare hour:** register with the Indian consulate.

The first city hall step matters most. Your SIM provider, your bank, and almost every other office will ask for a residence card with your address on the back.

## A few notes for each step

**Residence card.** Check that your name on the card matches your passport exactly. Indian names often differ in spelling, order, or the use of surnames across documents, and fixing a mismatch later is much harder than catching it now.

**Address registration.** Do this soon after moving in, ideally within 14 days. The city hall will ask for your full address, so have it written down, including the building name and room number.

**Health insurance.** It covers most of your medical costs, so you pay only a portion at the clinic. Always carry the card, and keep the monthly payments up to date.

**My Number.** Keep the letter safe. You will need it for the card application, and some banks and offices ask for the number itself.

**SIM and bank.** These two depend on each other. Many banks want a Japanese phone number, and many providers want a Japanese address. Read our [SIM provider guide](/posts/sim-providers) before choosing a plan. If you hold a scholarship, check with your funder before opening a bank account.

**Hanko or signature.** Japan relies on stamps (hanko) far more than India does, but most banks also accept a signature. Whichever you choose, use the same one every time.

**Consulate registration.** This is not a Japanese legal requirement, but it is worth doing. Visit the consulate's website for the current form and the list of documents. Confirm that your city falls under the Osaka-Kobe jurisdiction.

## Before you go to city hall

- Bring your residence card, passport, and student ID every time.
- Write your Japanese address and phone number on a sheet of paper.
- Ask your university's international office or a senior student whether they can come with you for the first visit, or whether an interpreter service is available at your ward office.
- Go on a weekday morning. Afternoons and the days after holidays tend to be crowded.

*Rules and counter procedures can differ between cities and change over time. Confirm the details with your city hall or ward office, and with your university's international office, before you go.*
