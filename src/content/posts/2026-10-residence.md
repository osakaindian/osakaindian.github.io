Finding a home is one of the first big tasks after arriving in Japan. Rooms in the university dormitories are limited, so many international students end up renting a private apartment. The earlier you start, the more choice you have.

## 🏠 Your main options

**1. University dormitories**
The cheapest option, usually with a furnished single room. Several dormitories are spread across the campuses, and some are shared with Japanese students. Applications are first-come, first-served when rooms are available.

**2. Private apartments**
The most common choice. You have more freedom and more choice, but higher costs and more paperwork. Co-op MyRoom (below) can help.

**3. Shared houses and other dormitories**
Shared houses have low upfront costs and are good for short stays. There are also dormitories run by outside organisations and private companies.

## 📍 Which area should you live in?

Osaka University has three campuses, and a free shuttle bus connects them.

* **Suita:** the largest campus, next to Expo ’70 Commemorative Park. It is home to Medicine, Dentistry, Pharmaceutical Sciences, Engineering, and Human Sciences. Many dormitories and apartments are nearby.
* **Toyonaka:** the campus for most first-year undergraduates, near Ishibashi-handaimae (Hankyu) and Shibahara-handaimae (Monorail) stations. It is a quiet residential area with plenty of student housing.
* **Minoh:** a smaller campus with direct access to central Osaka. It is home to the Graduate Schools of International Public Policy and Language and Culture.

Choose a place close to the campus where you will spend most of your time. If your classes are on more than one campus, check how close you are to the shuttle bus and the stations.

## 🛏️ University dormitories

The University of Osaka runs several dormitories for international students, including:

* Toneyama Dormitory and Seimei Dormitory (Toyonaka, shared with Japanese students)
* Suita International Student Dormitory
* Tsukumodai International Student Dormitory and Global Village Tsukumodai (Suita)
* International House Toyonaka (C wing)
* Niina International Dormitory (Minoh)

**How to apply**

* You can apply from **3 months before** your start date.
* Applications are accepted on a **first-come, first-served** basis when there are vacancies.
* You should receive an admission permit within about **2 weeks**.
* You can move in from about **5 days before** your entrance date.
* You can only stay while you are enrolled, and in March and September you must move out by the **25th**.
* You cannot apply for another university dormitory while you are living in one.

## 🤝 Co-op MyRoom: where to get help

The Osaka University Co-op runs **MyRoom**, a housing service for students, researchers, and staff. It has the largest number of properties around the campuses, including some furnished rooms. It has an English section for international students and researchers, English-speaking staff, and help arranging electricity, gas, water, and insurance.

If you rent through Co-op, you also get free help with setting up internet and utilities, registering your address at the city office, and opening a bank account. Without a Co-op contract, you can still use these services at a low cost.

## 📝 What you need to rent an apartment

Most landlords require two things:

* **Liability insurance.** The university recommends the *Comprehensive Insurance for Student Lives Coupled with PAS for International Students* (“Inbound Futai-Gakuso”), which also works as the tenant fire insurance.
* **A guarantor.** If you don't have one in Japan, the university recommends **Coop-Rent Insure**, a guarantor service for international students and researchers. You can arrange it through the real estate company or at a MyRoom counter. 📧 info@coop-ri.com

You will usually also need your passport, residence card (or a copy of your Certificate of Eligibility if you have not arrived yet), proof of enrolment, and your contact details. Each agent may ask for different documents, so check with them.

## ✈️ Before you come vs. after you arrive

**Before coming to Japan**

* Apply for a dormitory as early as you can.
* If you are not in a dormitory, contact MyRoom or a real estate agent early.
* Plan a few days of temporary accommodation (a hotel or guesthouse) in case your room is not ready.
* Prepare your insurance and guarantor arrangements.
* Avoid the busy seasons for apartments, mid-February to March and mid-August to September.

**After arriving**

* Visit the real estate agent or MyRoom to view rooms and sign the contract.
* Register your address at the city office (within 14 days of moving in).
* Set up electricity, gas, water, and internet.
* Learn the garbage rules, which can be strict.

## 💴 What does it actually cost?

The initial cost of renting a private apartment depends heavily on the property and the real estate agent. **You do not necessarily need to pay four to six months' rent upfront.** If you are on a student budget, look for apartments with **no key money (礼金なし / reikin nashi)** and compare the total move-in cost before deciding.

**Initial costs to check**

- **First month's rent:** Usually paid when signing the contract, sometimes with prorated rent for the first month.
- **Security deposit (敷金 / shikikin):** May be refundable after deductions for cleaning or damage, depending on the contract.
- **Key money (礼金 / reikin):** A non-refundable payment to the landlord. Many students try to avoid this by choosing a property advertised as *reikin nashi*.
- **Agent fee (仲介手数料):** Check how much the real estate agency charges.
- **Guarantor fee:** Usually required if you do not have a suitable guarantor in Japan.
- **Other charges:** Fire insurance, key exchange, initial cleaning, and any mandatory support or service fees.

**💡 Tips to save money**

- Search for **礼金なし (no key money)** and, if possible, **敷金なし (no security deposit)**.
- Ask MyRoom or the real estate agent for the **complete initial cost estimate** before committing. A cheap-looking apartment can have expensive additional fees.
- Compare the total upfront payment, not just the monthly rent. Also check whether cleaning and other fees are charged upfront or when you move out.
- Ask whether optional services can be declined, and check the contract carefully before signing.
- If possible, avoid moving during the busy February–March period, when competition for rooms is higher.

**Monthly costs**

* Rent, plus a small management or common-area fee
* Electricity, gas, water, and internet
* Contract renewal fees every couple of years for some properties
* Cleaning or restoration costs when you move out

As a rough guide, a small one-room apartment around Suita, Toyonaka, or Minoh may cost around **¥40,000–¥70,000 per month**, although the price varies with location, room condition, size, and facilities. Check current listings on Co-op MyRoom to compare actual options.

**The most important thing:** Before choosing an apartment, ask for a breakdown of everything you must pay before moving in. Compare two or three properties, and choose one that fits both your monthly budget and your initial budget.

## 🏡 What is a typical Japanese room like?

* **1R / 1K:** one room (1R), or one room with a separate kitchen (1K), the usual student layout
* **Size:** often 15–25 m², small by international standards
* **Facilities:** a compact kitchen, a unit bath (shower, bath, and toilet in one), and a small balcony
* **Furnishing:** usually **unfurnished**, with no bed, fridge, or washing machine, so budget for these or ask about furnished rooms
* **Air conditioning:** often included, but check

## 💡 A simple way to start

**Decide on a dorm or apartment → Apply early → Contact MyRoom → Arrange insurance and guarantor → Check all costs → Sign the contract → Set up utilities and register at the city office**

If you have questions, the University's Support Office and MyRoom staff are happy to help. When in doubt, ask before you sign anything.
